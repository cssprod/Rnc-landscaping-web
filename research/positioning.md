# RNC Landscaping: positioning (one page)

## Positioning statement
For homeowners, strata councils, property managers and institutional buyers in Greater Victoria, RNC Landscaping is one local contractor for grounds maintenance, landscape installation, irrigation and tree and vegetation work. We plan each job around the property and the local rules that apply to it, such as CRD watering schedules and municipal tree bylaws, and we tell clients clearly what we will do, when, and what happens next.

## Three messaging pillars
1. **One crew, four services.** Maintenance, installation, irrigation and tree work handled by one contractor and one point of contact, so nothing falls between trades. Proof: service pages, sample projects (labelled "Sample"), a single quote form for every service.
2. **Local rules, handled.** We check which watering rules and tree bylaws apply before we start and point clients to the official source. Proof: sourced resources, area pages with local notes, permit check named as a step in the process.
3. **Clear from quote to finish.** A short quote form, a stated next step, a written scope, and regular updates on recurring work. Proof: the quote flow itself, process sections on each service page, FAQ.

## Voice
- **Adjectives:** plain-spoken, dependable, neighbourly.
- **Style:** short sentences, Canadian spelling, concrete nouns (beds, hedges, sprinkler zones), say what we do rather than how good we are.
- **Words and phrases to avoid:** best, #1, leading, premier, top-rated, guaranteed results (unless [WARRANTY] defines it), award-winning, eco-friendly or green (unless a specific practice backs it), "years of experience", cheap, affordable, lowest price, any price or "starting at", "transform your outdoor oasis" and similar filler, legal-sounding promises about permits ("we'll get your permit approved").

## How to speak to each audience
**(a) Homeowners**
- Want: a tidy, healthy yard without managing it; a trustworthy person on their property; to avoid trouble with tree permits or watering fines.
- Say: what a visit includes, how scheduling works, that we check tree rules before cutting, and how to ask for a free quote. Use neighbourhood names on area pages.
- Avoid: jargon and contract language.

**(b) Strata councils and property managers**
- Want: consistent service across the season, predictable scope they can take to council, one accountable contact, documents on request (insurance, WorkSafeBC), quick response to owner complaints.
- Say: written scope and visit schedule, seasonal plan, how issues are reported and resolved, how we handle several sites, that we can quote to an existing specification. Address multi-unit details (complex address governs CRD lawn watering days).
- Avoid: residential warmth at the expense of specifics; claims about other stratas we cannot back.

**(c) Commercial, institutional and public buyers (municipalities, school districts, health and campus facilities, housing providers, churches and nonprofits, general contractors)**
- Want: compliance, safety and documentation; ability to work to a written specification or standard; clear subcontractor behaviour on construction sites; reliable scheduling around occupants.
- Say: we can respond to formal requests and specifications, provide insurance and safety documentation on request, coordinate with site supervisors, and work to the scope and schedule set by the owner or GC.
- Avoid: overstating capacity or credentials; anything that sounds like a price commitment before a site review. Never name past public clients unless real and approved.
